# SURP 2026: Replicator Dynamics and Quadratic Approximations for Nonconvex Optimization

This repository contains MATLAB experiments developed during a 2026 SURP research project at Cal Poly by Sam Williams and Dr. Brendon Anderson. The project studies nonconvex optimization through evolutionary game theory, with particular interest in the relationship between replicator dynamics, approximately Gaussian replicator flows (AGRFs), and local quadratic models of a nonconvex objective.

The current scripts focus on a finite-grid approximation of the strategy space. They simulate replicator dynamics for an objective function and compare the resulting population behavior with dynamics generated by both:

1. a fixed quadratic approximation centered at the known global minimizer, or
2. a time-varying quadratic approximation centered at the current population mean.

The main goal of these experiments is to understand when the dynamics generated by a nonconvex objective remain close to those generated by a quadratic model, and to identify situations in which a moving quadratic approximation succeeds or fails.

> **Note:** These scripts simulate finite-dimensional replicator dynamics on a discretized strategy grid. They are motivated by the AGRF framework, but they are not direct implementations of the closed-form Gaussian AGRF ODEs.

## Mathematical setup

For a discretized strategy set

$$
S = \{s_1,\ldots,s_n\},
$$

the population state is a probability vector

$$
x(t) \in \Delta^n,
$$

where $x_i(t)$ is the population mass assigned to strategy $s_i$.

For an objective function $g$, the scripts assign each strategy the excess payoff

$$
F_i(x)=\mathbb{E}_x[g]-g(s_i).
$$

Thus, strategies with smaller objective value receive larger payoff. The population evolves according to the replicator equation

$$
\dot{x}_i
=
x_i\left(F_i(x)-x^\top F(x)\right).
$$

In MATLAB, this is implemented in the form

```matlab
F = @(x) x' * g(S) - g(S);
f = @(t,x) x .* (F(x) - x' * F(x));
[t,x] = ode45(f,tspan,x0);
```

The population mean is computed by

$$
m(t)=\sum_i s_i x_i(t),
$$

and, in two dimensions, the covariance matrix is computed by

$$
C(t)
=
\sum_i (s_i-m(t))(s_i-m(t))^\top x_i(t)
=
S^\top\text{diag}(x(t))S-m(t)m(t)^\top.
$$

The largest eigenvalue $\lambda_{\max}(C(t))$ is used as a scalar measure of the population's spread.

## Quadratic models

Two types of quadratic comparison are used throughout the repository.

### Fixed quadratic approximation at the optimizer

First we compare the original objective $g$ with a fixed quadratic approximation $q_g$ centered at the known global minimizer $s^\star$.

This provides a baseline for the locally quadratic behavior expected near a strict minimum.

### Time-varying quadratic approximation

Then we construct a second-order Taylor approximation at the current population mean:

$$
q(m,s)
=
g(m)
+
\nabla g(m)^\top(s-m)
+
\frac{1}{2}(s-m)^\top \nabla^2 g(m)(s-m).
$$

Because $m=m(x)$ changes with the population state, the corresponding payoff function is also time varying. In the MATLAB scripts, only `g`, its gradient, and its Hessian need to be changed in order to define this approximation for a new objective.

## Repository files

### `convergence_1D.m`

One-dimensional convergence experiment using the quartic objective

$$
g(s)=\frac{3}{2}s^4-\frac{1}{4}s^3-3s^2+\frac{3}{4}s+1.
$$

The script compares three systems:

- replicator dynamics under the original objective `g`;
- replicator dynamics under a quadratic approximation `qg` centered at the global minimizer $s^\star=-1$; and
- replicator dynamics under a time-varying Taylor approximation `q` centered at the current mean.

It produces:

- surface plots of the evolving strategy distributions;
- mean trajectories for all three systems;
- an $\epsilon$-neighborhood around the global minimizer; and
- markers indicating the first time after which each trajectory remains inside that neighborhood.

The helper function `settlingIndex` identifies this settling time by locating the final tolerance violation.

### `convergence_2D_ST.m`

Two-dimensional Styblinski--Tang convergence experiment. The objective is

$$
g(s_1,s_2)
=
\frac{1}{2}\left(
 s_1^4-16s_1^2+5s_1
+s_2^4-16s_2^2+5s_2
\right)+80,
$$

with known global minimizer approximately

$$
s^\star=(-2.903534,-2.903534).
$$

The script compares the original objective, a fixed quadratic approximation at $s^\star$, and a time-varying Taylor approximation centered at the current mean.

It plots:

- all three mean trajectories over Styblinski--Tang contours;
- distance $\|m(t)-s^\star\|_2$ from the global minimizer;
- $\epsilon$-settling points for the three trajectories; and
- the evolution of $\lambda_{\max}(C(t))$ for each population.

This is the main two-dimensional convergence script for comparing the original and time-varying quadratic dynamics.

### `time_varying_fails_1D_V1.m`

One-dimensional failure-case experiment using the same quartic objective as `convergence_1D.m`.

The initial population is a narrow Gaussian-shaped distribution centered near the local minimum at $s=1$. The script compares the original objective, the quadratic approximation centered at the global minimizer, and the time-varying Taylor approximation.

This experiment is intended to show how a local, mean-centered quadratic model can get stuck in local minima and fail to reproduce the global behavior of the original replicator dynamics when the population is too concentrated in an unfavorable region.

### `time_varying_fails_1D_V2.m`

A second one-dimensional failure-case experiment. The strategy region is shifted so that the middle strategy falls on a local maximum and so a uniform initial distribution creates a different problematic case for the time-varying approximation.

The experiment is used to study behavior near unfavorable local curvature, including situations in which the moving quadratic dynamics can be strongly influenced by a local maximum.

### `time_varying_fails_2D_ST.m`

Two-dimensional Styblinski--Tang failure-case experiment.

The script uses the same objective, gradient, Hessian, and three-way comparison as `convergence_2D_ST.m`, but with a different initial distribution. It is intended to test a region in which the time-varying Taylor approximation does not track the original dynamics as successfully.

Outputs include:

Together, `convergence_2D_ST.m` and `time_varying_fails_2D_ST.m` provide contrasting two-dimensional examples of when the moving quadratic approximation does and does not capture the behavior of the original system.

### `Three_Hump_Camel_2D.m`

Two-dimensional experiment using the three-hump camel function

$$
g(s_1,s_2)
=
2s_1^2-1.05s_1^4+\frac{s_1^6}{6}+s_1s_2+s_2^2.
$$

The global minimizer is at the origin, and the script also marks the two nonglobal local minima.

The script compares the original objective, a fixed quadratic approximation at the mimizer, and a time-varying Taylor approximation centered at the current mean.

### `Rastrigin_2D.m`

Two-dimensional experiment using the Rastrigin function

$$
g(s_1,s_2)
=
20+s_1^2-10\cos(2\pi s_1)+s_2^2-10\cos(2\pi s_2).
$$

The global minimizer is $s^\star=(0,0)$. Because Rastrigin contains many local minima, this experiment tests the comparison between replicator dynamics and a local quadratic model on a highly oscillatory landscape.

The script compares the original objective, a fixed quadratic approximation at the mimizer, and a time-varying Taylor approximation centered at the current mean.

## What the experiments measure

Across the repository, the main quantities of interest are:

- **Population state** `x(t)`: probability mass assigned to each discretized strategy.
- **Mean trajectory** `m(t)`: the average strategy selected by the evolving population.
- **Global-optimizer error** $\|m(t)-s^\star\|_2$.
- **Settling time**: the first time after which a mean trajectory remains inside a prescribed $\epsilon$-neighborhood of the optimizer.
- **Covariance matrix** `C(t)`: the spread and orientation of the population distribution.
- **Maximum covariance eigenvalue** $\lambda_{\max}(C(t))$: the largest directional variance of the population.

These quantities make it possible to compare not only the final optimizer approached by each system, but also the transient behavior of the mean and the concentration of the population.

## Adjustable parameters

Most experiments can be modified from the definitions near the beginning of each file.

Common parameters include:

- `S` -- strategy interval and grid spacing;
- `m0` -- initial population mean;
- `sigma0` -- width of the Gaussian-shaped initial distribution;
- `x0` -- initial probability distribution;
- `tspan` -- ODE simulation interval;
- `g` -- objective function;
- `gradG` -- gradient of the objective;
- `hessianG` -- Hessian of the objective;
- `sStar` -- known global minimizer; and
- `epsilon` -- convergence neighborhood used in settling-time plots.

For the time-varying approximation experiments, the Taylor model is constructed from `g`, its gradient, and its Hessian. This makes it possible to reuse the same dynamics code for other sufficiently smooth objectives by changing those function definitions.

## Numerical considerations

All experiments use MATLAB's `ode45` to integrate the replicator equation.

The strategy space is discretized, so grid resolution directly affects both accuracy and computational cost. In one dimension, reducing the grid spacing increases the number of states linearly. In two dimensions, the number of strategies grows quadratically with the number of grid points along each axis, and the ODE state dimension grows accordingly.

The initial Gaussian-shaped distributions are normalized as discrete probability vectors:

```matlab
x0 = exp(-dist2/(2*sigma0^2));
x0 = x0/sum(x0);
```

The two-dimensional covariance calculation assumes each row of `strategies` is one strategy vector:

```matlab
C = strategies' * (strategies .* x) - m*m';
```

Clone the repository, open MATLAB in the repository directory, and run any of the scripts directly. For example:

```matlab
convergence_2D_ST
```

## Research context

The experiments are motivated by the evolutionary-game-theoretic formulation of nonconvex optimization used in *Approximately Gaussian Replicator Flows: Nonconvex Optimization as a Nash-Convergent Evolutionary Game*. In that framework, minimization is recast as a population game whose payoffs reward strategies with lower objective values, and replicator dynamics provide the associated evolutionary process.

The current repository investigates a related local-approximation question: if a smooth nonconvex objective is replaced by its quadratic Taylor model along the evolving mean trajectory, how closely do the resulting replicator dynamics track the original system? The convergence and failure-case scripts are designed to provide numerical evidence for the assumptions and bounds needed to analyze that question.
