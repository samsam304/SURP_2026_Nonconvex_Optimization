# SURP 2026: Nonconvex Optimization with Replicator Dynamics

This repository contains MATLAB experiments developed during a 2026 SURP
research project at Cal Poly by Sam Williams and Dr. Brendon Anderson. The
project studies nonconvex optimization through the evolutionary-game-theoretic
framework of approximately Gaussian replicator flows (AGRFs).

## Project overview

In the AGRF framework, an optimization problem is interpreted as a population
game in which candidate solutions act as strategies. The payoff assigned to a
strategy is its improvement relative to the population-average objective
value. Consequently, strategies with smaller objective values gain population
mass under the replicator dynamics.

The experiments in this repository discretize the strategy space into a finite
grid and numerically integrate the resulting replicator equation with
MATLAB's `ode45` solver. For each nonconvex objective `g`, the dynamics are
compared with those generated by a quadratic approximation `q`.

The primary quantities of interest are:

- the evolution of the probability distribution over strategies;
- the population mean trajectory `m(t)`;
- the distance between the mean and the known global minimizer;
- the covariance matrix and its largest eigenvalue; and
- the difference between the trajectories generated by the original and
  quadratic objectives.

These comparisons are intended to illustrate when a nonconvex objective can
be locally represented by a quadratic model and how the accuracy of that
representation changes as the population distribution becomes concentrated.

## Experiment files

### `Approximation1.m`

One-dimensional experiment using the quartic objective

$$
f(x)=\frac{3}{2}x^4-\frac{1}{4}x^3-3x^2+\frac{3}{4}x+1.
$$

The script compares the distribution and mean trajectories under `f` with
those under the quadratic approximation centered at the global minimizer
`x* = -1`.

### `Approximation2.m`

Two-dimensional Styblinski--Tang experiment. The script compares mean
trajectories from multiple initial conditions, plots them over the objective
contours, measures convergence to the global minimizer, and tracks the largest
eigenvalue of the covariance matrix.

### `Approximation3.m`

Two-dimensional three-hump camel experiment. This example includes both
global and nonglobal local minima and tests whether the original and
quadratic dynamics exhibit similar behavior near those points.

### `Approximation4.m`

Two-dimensional Rastrigin experiment. The Rastrigin objective contains many
local minima and is used to examine the behavior of the replicator dynamics
on a highly nonconvex and oscillatory landscape.

### `TimeVaryingApprox1.m`

One-dimensional experiment using a time-varying quadratic approximation.
At each time, the quadratic is constructed by expanding the original
objective around the current mean of the population

$$
q(m,s)
=
g(m)
+
\nabla g(m)^\top (s-m)
+
\frac{1}{2} (s-m)^\top \nabla^2 g(m)(s-m).
$$

The resulting mean trajectory is compared with the mean generated by the
original objective.

## Adjustable parameters

The most important experiment settings are located near the beginning of
each script:

- `S`: strategy range and grid resolution;
- `x0`: initial population state;
- `startingPoints`: initial means for the two-dimensional experiments;
- `sigma0`: spread of the initial Gaussian-shaped population;
- `tspan`: simulated time interval;
- the definitions of the objective and quadratic approximation;
- `sStar`: known global minimizer; and
- `ode45` error tolerances supplied through `odeset`.

A finer grid generally gives a better approximation of the continuous
strategy space, but substantially increases computation time. This is
especially important in two dimensions, where reducing the grid spacing
causes the number of strategies to grow quadratically.

## Requirements

- MATLAB

## Running an experiment

Open one of the `.m` files in MATLAB and run the script. Each script defines
its own objective, initial conditions, numerical integration settings, and
plots.

The one-dimensional scripts produce plots of the evolving strategy
distribution and mean trajectory. The two-dimensional scripts additionally
produce contour plots, convergence-error plots, and covariance-eigenvalue
plots.
